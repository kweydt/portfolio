00 // WHAT I'M THINKING ABOUT

Enterprise software is mid-earthquake. The lines between design, product, and the people who build it are getting blurry.

My take: UX leaders win the next decade by tightening feedback loops and laying down artifacts that scale — design systems, rules, gates — so quality is the default outcome even when no designer is in the room.

→ NOT DONE WORKING IT OUT. THAT'S WHERE MY CHIPS ARE.

01 // ABOUT

Rural Montana, graduating class of 32. Stanford product design. Eighteen years across startup & enterprise, IC to 40-person org, design to product management. At small companies someone had to wear the extra hats — I tended to volunteer.

Two acquisitions. Covered a VP role for a 40-person UX org during a leadership transition. Currently leading UX for a platform that handles integration plumbing for a meaningful slice of the Fortune 500. The work that's mine to claim is the strategy, the team, and the judgment calls — the execution belongs to the people I've been lucky to lead.

What I try to bring: curiosity, a low tolerance for buzzwords, opinionated empathy, and a willingness to be wrong out loud.
