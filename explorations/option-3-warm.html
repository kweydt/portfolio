What I'm thinking about

A working POV, not a finished one.

Enterprise software is mid-earthquake. The lines between design, product, and the people who build it are getting blurry — in ways that make a lot of senior leaders nervous, and that I find genuinely interesting.

My take: UX leaders win the next decade by tightening feedback loops and laying down artifacts that scale — design systems, rules, gates — so quality is the default outcome even when no designer is in the room.

Not done working it out, but that's where my chips are.

A bit about me

The short version, written by the only person willing to.

I grew up in rural Montana — graduating class of 32 — and went to Stanford for product design. The combination of "small town" and "design school" turned out to be useful: I learned early that the people using your product usually have something better to do, and that's not their problem to fix.

The last ~18 years have taken me across more disciplines than I planned — design, product management, a little front-end, marketing, support, QA. At small companies someone had to wear the extra hats, and I tended to volunteer. Two of my companies were acquired. I covered a VP role for a 40-person UX org during a leadership transition. The work that's mine to claim is the strategy, the team, and the judgment calls; the execution belongs to the people I've been lucky to lead.

What I try to bring is curiosity, a low tolerance for buzzwords, opinionated empathy, and a willingness to be wrong out loud. I still review pixels, still write the strategy doc, still ship the occasional bit of production code (this site, for one) — though I'd never call myself an engineer.
