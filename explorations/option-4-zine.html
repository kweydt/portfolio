What I'm thinking about

Enterprise software is mid-earthquake. The lines between design, product, and the people who build it are getting blurry — in ways that make a lot of senior leaders nervous, and that I find genuinely interesting.

My take: UX leaders win the next decade by tightening feedback loops and laying down artifacts that scale — design systems, rules, gates — so quality is the default outcome even when no designer is in the room.

Not done working it out, but that's where my chips are.

About me, briefly

I grew up in rural Montana — graduating class of 32 — and studied product design at Stanford. The last eighteen years have taken me across more disciplines than I planned: design, product management, a little front-end, marketing, support, QA. At small companies someone had to wear the extra hats, and I tended to volunteer.

Two acquisitions. Covered a VP role for a 40-person UX org during a leadership transition. Currently leading UX for a platform that handles integration plumbing for a meaningful chunk of the Fortune 500. The work that's mine to claim is the strategy, the team, and the judgment calls — the execution belongs to the people I've been lucky to lead.

What I try to bring is curiosity, a low tolerance for buzzwords, opinionated empathy, and a willingness to be wrong out loud. Still review pixels. Still write the strategy doc. Still ship the occasional bit of production code (this site, for one) — though I'd never call myself an engineer.
